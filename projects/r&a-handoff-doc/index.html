    Context

    What the Requests & Approval tab does today, how a request travels through it and where it hurts. Taken from the key facts of the Nov 20 walkthrough meeting.

    What this tab does
    • It is the Project Management section of the Fever Zone partner portal: one place for every request Fever sends to a partner.
    • Two kinds of item. Info requests: the partner fills in information only they hold. Validations (approvals): Fever holds the information and the partner approves it before it goes to production.
    • They stay separate because the partner's action is different.
    • Requests flow only from Fever to partners. Partners cannot open requests to Fever yet.
    • Owners are Ops PMs and Marketing. It is mostly aimed at Ops teams handling complex deals.
    • Pending items are listed in a tracker, filterable by type and status, with launch and dates.

    Why it exists
    • Built a few months ago in a rush, by one engineer and without design, because PM demand was high and partners were not getting timely responses.
    • Goal: PMs stop requesting everything by email. Partner-opened requests come later.
    • Vision: a single two-way channel for all Fever-partner requests, with one to-do list by launch and approval and a notification bell.
    • A launch groups plans (experiences between two dates). Step 1 of every request is linking it to a launch, because Ops, marketing campaigns and invoicing work by launch, not by plan.
    • Usage is growing every month, and Marketing now sends almost all marketing requests, which was the main goal.

    The request types

    Info requests

    • Templates are grouped under Listing, Marketing and Other.
    • Plan creation sends the partner to Create Event > Quick Setup. It replaces a flow where the partner emailed the PM, who re-typed everything in a Google Sheet that fed a Jira ticket and then Salesforce.
    • Marketing requests work the same way without the plan link and cover general content or a specific campaign. The form holds every field ever needed, and hiding fields avoids asking twice for reusable content.
    • Steps: launch, details (title, due date, Required/Optional/Hide per question), alerts (reminders by frequency, timezone and hour, partner contacts, email language, Slack) and a review summary.
    • Partners upload from local files, a Google Drive picker or a shared link. Uploads mirror to a shared Drive for Creative.

    Approvals

    • Plan approvals need a title, plan URL and validator.
    • Marketing approvals are the most complex, a 7-step flow with campaign and material type. Assets go through Ziflow for pin-point image feedback, and validating there marks the Fever Zone item completed.
    • Landing, CRM and media approvals take any number of URLs, a title and a due date. Each item is "approve" or "FYI only".
    • Copy approvals are texts written on the partner's behalf (plan listings, CRM campaigns). Marketing keeps its Google Sheets templates, mirrored into the request by a service account. Partners approve or reject each row, and the result syncs back to the sheet.
    • The detail page shows the item, its assets and Approve / Disapprove. Every request has a Change history tab, and each item has its own comments with @-mentions.

    What partners struggle with
    • Screens are unclear and not clean. Negative feedback is about clarity, not about usage.
    • Session recordings show about half of partner visitors do not understand the tool and leave.
    • Main complaint: the approval asset table scrolls sideways but looks cut off, hiding content.
    • The partner copy table has little usable space and is unusable on mobile. Copy approvals are the most critical and urgent to redesign.
    • Long contact lists are painful to search.
    • Heavy senders re-select the same ~5 recipients every time.
    • The email link errors unless the right organization is already selected.
    • No central place holds all of a partner's assets. They live per request.
    • URL feedback is comments only: partners cannot point at elements or attach images.
    • After sending, only recipients and validators can be changed. Anything else means recreating the request.

    Known bugs and limitsOpen
    • Resent copy rows stay "Rejected" and the sheet status does not reset to pending.
    • Uploading a new version wrongly moves the Fever Zone request back to pending.
    • The Slack channel dropdown shows the creator's channels to everyone.
    • The Google Drive picker failed with an error during the demo.
    • Ziflow activity is not synced into the Change history.
    • Fever cannot change the partner-side status directly. A V2 should not show pending until it is ready.
    • Requests can be cancelled and reactivated but not deleted, because emailed links would break.
    • "Resend for validation" is planned but not live. Approving in Ziflow does not reactivate the request automatically yet.

    Where it is heading
    • Next step is a UX redesign so the tool is clear and matches the rest of Fever Zone. No big new features are planned, only tweaks, so it is a good moment for a full UX pass.
    • Interview users first and fix obvious UI issues in parallel. Deeper changes wait for usage insights.
    • Redesign goal for partners: a simpler UX, with filters and search on the request list.
    • The dynamic plan-creation form is retired next month in favour of a partner flow that creates plans directly (pilot going well).
    • Open: scope of the redesign, and how request permissions fit with role-based permissions.
    • Open: an intermediate "under review" status instead of complete-and-reopen, to match Ziflow.
    • Open: should whole requests ever be deletable?
    • Open: how to get contextual feedback on landing pages.
    • Ziflow work is paused. A move to another proofing tool is likely in the coming months.
